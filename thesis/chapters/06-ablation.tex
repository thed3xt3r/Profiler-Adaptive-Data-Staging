\section{Scratch capacity: the regime the design targets}
\label{sec:ablation-capacity}

The Bing 1k corpus fits comfortably in node-local scratch, which is the least
interesting case for an adaptive stager: a fixed policy of ``copy everything up
front'' suffices.
The design is aimed at the regime where it does not fit, and that regime is reached
here by synthetically constraining the scratch budget.

\begin{table}[!ht]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Scratch budget (\% of corpus) & Derived depth $d$ & Throughput & Staging hit rate \\
\midrule
100\% & & & \\
 50\% & & & \\
 25\% & & & \\
 10\% & & & \\
  0\% (staging unavailable) & --- & & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Behaviour as scratch capacity is constrained. Method~4 of
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-plan} is unavailable below 100\%; \pads{} should
degrade gracefully rather than fail. \PLACEHOLDER{populate}}
\label{tab:ablation-scratch}
\end{table}

This is the experiment that distinguishes a contribution from an observation.
If \pads{} matches full pre-staging at 100\% and retains most of its advantage at
25\%, the adaptivity is doing work.
If it collapses to baseline as soon as the corpus does not fit, the honest reading
is that the method is a convenient wrapper around ``copy the dataset to scratch''.

\section{Classifier threshold sensitivity}
\label{sec:ablation-thresholds}

The classification rules of Section~\ref{ch:methodology} depend on two thresholds,
$\gamma_s$ and $\gamma_d$, set to 1.25 and 1.5 respectively.
Values chosen by the designer and never varied are a standing weakness in any
adaptive system, and the sensitivity is reported here.

\begin{table}[!ht]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
$\gamma_d$ & Policy selected & Throughput & Correct decision? \\
\midrule
1.0 & & & \\
1.25 & & & \\
1.5 (default) & & & \\
2.0 & & & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Sensitivity of the policy decision to the classification threshold.
\PLACEHOLDER{populate}}
\label{tab:ablation-thresholds}
\end{table}

The relevant question is not which threshold maximises throughput on this workload
--- that would be tuning on the test set --- but how wide the band is over which the
decision is unchanged.
A decision that flips under a 10\% perturbation of $\gamma_d$ is not robust.

\section{Shuffling randomness}
\label{sec:ablation-shuffle}

Shard-based reading trades exact random access for sequential throughput, and
approximates shuffling with a sample buffer.
This ablation varies the buffer size to establish where, if anywhere, reduced
shuffling randomness begins to affect segmentation quality.
