\section{Pipeline Defects Found and Corrected}
\label{app:defects}
\addcontentsline{lot}{section}{Pipeline Defects Found and Corrected}
%% ============================================================================

Porting the training code to a second environment surfaced a number of defects in
the experimental pipeline. This appendix records them, what each one did to the
measurements, and how it was resolved.

Two things are worth stating at the outset, because the length of the list
invites a misreading. First, almost all of these defects are \emph{silent}: they
degrade results or invalidate measurements without raising an error, which is
precisely why they survive casual testing and why porting to a second
environment is an effective way to find them. A pipeline that produced plausible
numbers throughout was, in several respects, not measuring what it reported.
Second, all but the few explicitly identified below as outstanding were fixed,
and every result reported in Sections~\ref{ch:results} and~\ref{ch:ablation} was
collected after those fixes were in place -- the evaluation sweep post-dates the
corrections by a week. This appendix is therefore a record of defects found and
closed, not a list of caveats attaching to the results.

It is included because reproduction is the mechanism that surfaced these
problems, and because the same defects are the sort that any reimplementation of
this workload would be liable to repeat. Documenting them is more useful to a
reader attempting that than a clean narrative would be.

\subsection{Staging mechanism: three defects, all corrected}

\subsubsection{Policy selected after the datasets it selects were constructed}
\label{sec:defect-ordering}

The staging policy was applied only to \code{DataLoader} construction, not to
dataset construction.
In the original control flow, the three datasets were built before the profiler ran:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
policy = base_config.get("data_policy", "loose")   # key absent -> "loose"
train_dataset = create_dataset(..., policy=policy) # always the loose reader
...
base_config["data_policy"] = policy_summary["policy"]  # profiler decides, too late
train_loader = build_dataloader(train_dataset, policy=base_config["data_policy"])
\end{lstlisting}

The datasets were never rebuilt after the profiler had spoken, and the selected
policy reached only \code{build\_dataloader}, whose entire use of it is to choose
\code{num\_workers} and \code{prefetch\_factor}.
The shard and stage reader classes were therefore unreachable code, and every run
- irrespective of the policy the profiler reported - read loose files.

The fix reorders policy selection ahead of dataset construction: the profiler
probes an explicitly-loose dataset, and the working datasets are then built from
its verdict, so the selected reader is the one actually used. Staging results
collected before this change describe a policy \emph{decision} rather than a
policy \emph{execution}, and were superseded by the re-run campaign reported in
this thesis.

\subsubsection{No archive the shard reader could open}

The shard reader addresses members by original filename within archives named
\code{originals.tar} and \code{negs.tar}.
The repository's shard generator produces WebDataset-format shards
(\code{shard-000000.tar}, members keyed \code{train\_000123.jpg}), which that reader
can neither open nor address; and no archives of either form existed on disk.
The shard branch would therefore have fallen through to its loose fallback even had
it been reachable.

Archives in the required layout are now produced by a dedicated builder, which
verifies its output by comparing decoded samples against the loose path.
They are written uncompressed for the reason given in
Section~\ref{ch:methodology}: the payload is already-compressed JPEG and PNG, so
compression yields negligible size reduction while forcing decompression from the
stream start on every random access.

\subsubsection{Staging silently degraded to a plain read}

The staging dataset copied samples to a scratch directory while swallowing
\code{IOError} and \code{OSError}. Because the scratch directory was never
created, every copy failed silently and the reader fell back to the source path,
appearing selected and active while having no effect. The directory is now
created up front and copy failures are surfaced rather than absorbed.

\subsection{A measurement consideration: profiling perturbs the training RNG}

One further interaction is worth recording, less as a defect than as a
constraint on how two runs may be compared. The warm-up pass iterates the
\emph{training} dataset, whose augmentation transforms are stochastic, and so
advances the global random state before training proper begins. A profiled run
is therefore not seed-identical to an unprofiled one at otherwise identical
configuration.

The effect is small and was quantified rather than assumed: repeated runs differ
by approximately $\pm 0.02$ IoU, which bounds the comparison. Either the random
state is restored after profiling, or differences below that magnitude between a
profiled and an unprofiled run are treated as run-to-run variance rather than as
an effect of profiling. The latter convention is used throughout this thesis.

A small number of purely operational issues encountered during the work --
checkpoint filenames interpolating a metric name containing a path separator,
and hyperparameter studies not persisting across an interrupted sweep -- are
omitted here. They cost time but have no bearing on the validity of any
measurement reported.

\subsection{Further issues found during the evaluation campaign}
\label{sec:defects-session2}

Four further defects were identified while running the full evaluation sweep.
The first two are infrastructure failures rather than correctness bugs, but are
documented because they caused real job losses and shaped the retry pattern
visible in some of the job identifiers referenced in Sections~\ref{ch:results}
and~\ref{ch:ablation}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Scratch directory scoped to \code{project\_root}, not shared.}
    \code{resolve\_scratch\_dir()} derived the node-local staging path from each
    job's \code{project\_root}, which is deliberately unique per experiment
    configuration for checkpoint isolation. Because every \code{stage}-policy
    job across the evaluation sweep therefore had its own scratch directory, each
    of the roughly forty such jobs independently copied its own multi-gigabyte
    snapshot of the dataset, accumulating 69 redundant copies ($\approx$91\,GB)
    and exhausting the account's disk quota mid-sweep, which in turn failed every
    subsequent job attempting to write output. A real node-local scratch mount is
    shared per node regardless of which job is using it; the fix decouples
    \code{scratch\_dir} from \code{project\_root} and defaults to one fixed,
    shared location unless explicitly overridden.
  \item \textbf{Checkpoint auto-resume from a truncated file.} Several jobs that
    had progressed far enough to begin writing \code{last.ckpt} before the disk
    quota was exhausted left a truncated checkpoint behind. Lightning resumes
    training from \code{last.ckpt} by default if one is present in the
    checkpoint directory, and loading a truncated \code{.ckpt} raises
    \code{RuntimeError: PytorchStreamReader failed reading zip archive} rather
    than falling back to a fresh run. Affected job directories were identified
    and their partial checkpoints removed before resubmission.
  \item \textbf{\code{0-reproduction}'s epoch-end hooks never wired in for
    two of three architectures.} \code{build\_model()} and
    \code{load\_model\_from\_checkpoint()} instantiated the plain
    \code{ArcheoModel} base class for the MA-Net and DeepLabV3+ code paths,
    rather than \code{\_EpochBufferedArcheoModel}, the subclass that implements
    \code{on\_\{train,validation,test\}\_epoch\_end} in place of the
    Lightning~1.x \code{*\_epoch\_end(self, outputs)} hooks the ported notebook
    code assumes (Lightning~2.x removed these; see also
    Section~\ref{sec:repro-faithfulness}). Only the SegFormer path used the
    correct subclass. Training completed without error under the plain base
    class -- per-step loss logging does not depend on the missing hook -- so the
    defect was silent until the test loop's aggregated metric lookup raised
    \code{KeyError: 'test/IOU-img'}, since the epoch-aggregated IoU had never
    actually been logged. Both non-SegFormer paths now use the buffered
    subclass; this was confirmed by a full run reaching the expected metric
    before the Table~\ref{tab:repro-full} results were collected.
  \item \textbf{Profiler warm-up window bias -- implementation diverged from its
    own documented design.} Documented in full in
    Section~\ref{sec:res-profiler-bias} rather than repeated here: the default
    8-batch profiling window included one-time CUDA/cuDNN warm-up cost in its
    \Tgpu{} average, inflating it 6--10$\times$ and systematically suppressing
    the adaptive policy across most of Section~\ref{ch:ablation}'s threshold
    sweep. The design had anticipated this failure mode:
    Section~\ref{ch:methodology} specifies discarding the first $w_0=10$
    batches and aggregating by median rather than mean, for precisely this
    reason. The divergence was between that specification and the shipped
    \code{profile\_data\_pipeline()}, which implemented neither safeguard --
    which is also why the fix was straightforward once the symptom was
    traced. Discard is now implemented and validated
    (\code{--sweep\_discard}), recovering full-epoch measurement accuracy at
    negligible cost. Median aggregation remains untested: a median is
    inherently less sensitive than a mean to a small number of outlying
    warm-up samples, so it may add robustness independent of discarding, but
    that has not been measured and is not claimed here.
    Included in this list because, unlike the first three defects, it is a
    correctness defect in \pads{} itself rather than in the evaluation
    infrastructure, and it directly affects how several results in
    Sections~\ref{ch:results} and~\ref{ch:ablation} should be interpreted.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implication for the results reported in this thesis}

Section~\ref{sec:defect-ordering} and the missing archives were the consequential
defects. Because the selected policy never reached the data path, and because the
shard policy had no readable data in any case, any staging result collected
before those fixes describes a policy \emph{decision} rather than a policy
\emph{execution}. Such results are not recoverable by reanalysis; the
experiments had to be re-run.

They were. Both defects were corrected before the evaluation campaign reported
in this thesis was carried out, and the full sweep was then collected against
the fixed pipeline. Every figure in Sections~\ref{ch:results}
and~\ref{ch:ablation} therefore describes policies that actually executed, with
the shard and stage readers reachable and supplied with archives in a layout
they can open. The measurements that motivated this appendix have been
superseded rather than merely annotated, and no result presented in this thesis
rests on them.

One qualification belongs here rather than in the main text. That the staging
policies now execute does not mean the adaptive layer routinely selects them:
Section~\ref{sec:res-profiler-bias} establishes that the profiler's warm-up
measurement was biased in a way that suppressed staging across most of the
threshold sweep, which is a separate defect with a separate resolution. The
distinction matters for reading Section~\ref{ch:ablation}: where a run reports
\code{loose}, that is now a decision the profiler genuinely made on the evidence
available to it, not an artefact of the selected policy failing to reach the
data path.

Finally, these defects are properties of the code rather than of any particular
machine, and applied equally to workstation and cluster runs. That is what makes
them worth documenting: none would have been caught by running the same code
again on the same hardware.
This appendix records defects discovered during reproduction and evaluation,
distinguishing corrections that affect staging behaviour from those that affect
segmentation accuracy or measurement validity.
